Multiplex fluorescence microscopy

Every pixel of the scan, at full resolution.

FluoroView opens multi-gigabyte multiplex scans on your own computer, shows the raw 16-bit values under the cursor, and measures regions on the original pixels with ImageJ's conventions.

Install Read the docs Source on GitHub

What it does

Measurement first. Everything else in service of it.

01

Full-resolution viewing

Zoom levels are built once from the raw data. A badge says when you see the scan's own pixels, and from 3,200% each pixel prints its value.

02

Channels you can trust

Exact histograms, display range and gamma per channel, a clipping highlight, a channel gallery locked to one view, and a pixel loupe.

03

ImageJ's measurements

Area, perimeter, fitted ellipse, circularity, solidity, Feret diameters and per-channel statistics up to kurtosis and integrated density, with background subtraction.

04

Exact regions

Rectangles, ellipses, polygons and freehand shapes. Union, intersection, XOR and subtraction; enlarge or shrink by microns; holes and multi-part regions.

05

Cell Counter

Click to count cells by category. Counts and densities per square millimetre for each region, and every point's position, export as CSV.

06

Line profiles

Intensity of every channel along a line, sampled from the right zoom level and exported with the scale and version it came from.

07

Sessions

One .fv file keeps regions, notes, counts, display settings, scale and view. It reopens onto the same image, checked by a content fingerprint.

08

Publication figures

Composite and per-channel panels with scale bar and labels, as PNG or TIFF at your DPI, or the raw 16-bit area as OME-TIFF.

09

ImageJ and QuPath

Regions go to and from ImageJ as .roi or RoiSet.zip and QuPath as GeoJSON, holes and multi-part regions included.

Local by design

Your scans stay on your computer.

The engine runs on your machine and the studio opens in your browser. Nothing is uploaded: the engine listens only on your own computer and answers only requests that carry the random token it prints at start-up.

Formats
TIFF and OME-TIFF, as one file or one file per channel
Pixels
Raw values kept end to end: tiles, statistics and exports
Cache
Zoom levels built once per scan and reused, 20 GB at most by default
Measured
A 3.87 GB four-channel scan on an M2 MacBook Pro: zoom levels in 8.5 s, then 0.1–0.9 ms per tile
Requires
Python 3.11 or newer, and a browser with WebGL 2

Quick start

Open a scan with File › Open… (⌘O), or start FluoroView with its path. Turn channels on and off with 1–9; A sets every channel's display range from its histogram. Press R and drag to draw a rectangle: its measurements appear beside it. ⌘E exports every region's measurements as CSV, and ⌘S saves the whole session.

Channels and display

Each channel has its exact histogram over the whole scan. The display never changes the data: statistics and exports always use the raw values.

  • Levels. Drag the black, midtone and white handles on the histogram, or type Min, Max and γ.
  • Colour. The swatch beside the channel name offers fluorescence colours, any colour, the colour maps Grays, Fire, Ice, Viridis, Magma and Inferno, and Invert.
  • Adjust. Brightness and Contrast move and narrow the display window, as in ImageJ; Intensity lowers the channel's output level; Curves shapes its tone curve: click to add a point, drag to shape, double-click to remove.
  • Add or Max. Beside Auto contrast: channels are summed, or each pixel shows its brightest channel.

Display settings are saved with the scan and in sessions, and exported figures use exactly the same mapping.

  • 1–9 show or hide a channel; ⇧1–9 show only that channel.
  • C highlights clipped pixels; ⇧L draws histograms on a log scale.
  • G opens the channel gallery: each visible channel beside the composite, at the same position and zoom.
  • Z shows the pixel loupe; M the minimap; ⇧G a pixel grid from 800%.

The badge at the bottom right reads Native when the screen shows the scan's own pixels. From 3,200%, each pixel prints its raw value for the visible channels; the view goes up to 12,800%.

Drawing tools return to V Move after each shape. In Move, drag a region to move it and its handles to reshape it; the arrow keys nudge it by one pixel (⇧: ten). Double-click a region to zoom to it; ⌫ deletes it, with Undo in the notice. Space-drag or middle-drag pans while drawing.

A pixel belongs to a region when its centre lies inside it. Rectangles are half-open, so neighbouring regions never share a pixel; polygons follow the even-odd rule.

Measurements

The card beside the selected region shows its area, shape and each visible channel's mean and SD, computed on the raw full-resolution pixels; hover a channel for the rest. Use as background makes a region the background: its mean is subtracted in the CSV's mean_minus_background column.

  • Perimeter is exact for rectangles and polygons (every outline, holes included) and uses Ramanujan's approximation for ellipses.
  • The fitted ellipse comes from the pixels' second moments, scaled to their area, as in ImageJ's Fit Ellipse.
  • Angles run from 0 to 180° with y pointing up, as in ImageJ.
  • Circularity is 4π·area / perimeter², roundness 4·area / (π·major²), solidity area / convex hull area.
  • Skewness and kurtosis are population moments; kurtosis is excess kurtosis (0 for a normal distribution). Ties for the mode go to the lowest value.
  • sum is ImageJ's RawIntDen; int_den is area × mean, in µm² when the scale is known.

Region operations

Select several regions with ⇧-click in the image or ⇧/⌘-click in the Regions list; ⌘A selects all. The Region menu, the command palette and the bar above the list then offer:

Each result is a new region named after its sources, such as “Tissue − Lumen”; the originals are kept. Results can have holes and several parts, and move as a whole.

ImageJ and QuPath

Region › Import regions (ImageJ, QuPath)…, or Import in the Regions tab, reads an ImageJ .roi or RoiSet.zip, or a QuPath GeoJSON file. Its regions are added to the scan's regions and selected. Export ImageJ RoiSet (.zip) and Export QuPath GeoJSON in the same menu write every region.

Rotated rectangles and ImageJ ellipses arrive as polygons, and curved composite outlines are followed closely. Lines, points, text and QuPath detections are not regions: an import leaves them out and says how many.

Cell Counter

Press K and click cells to count them into the current category; the first click creates “Type 1”. ⌥-click removes the nearest point, and Esc ends counting. Categories are listed under Counts in the Regions tab: click one to make it current, double-click its name to rename it, or change its colour. Points CSV lists every point; Counts CSV gives counts and densities for each category in each region.

Line profile

Press L and drag a line (⇧ snaps to 45°). The drawer below the image plots every channel along it, with up to 2,048 samples read from the finest zoom level that provides them. Hover the chart to see the position on the image, and export the values as CSV.

Scale

FluoroView uses the pixel size recorded in the file. To set it yourself, draw a line along a scale bar, choose Image › Set scale… and enter the bar's length. The scale is then used in every µm column, scale bar, profile, figure and session; Remove scale returns to the file's value.

Notes

Press N and click to pin a note to a place in the scan; others can reply to it. Notes are listed in the Regions tab and saved with the session.

Sessions

⌘S saves a .fv file: the regions, notes, counts, display settings, scale, view, line profile and export settings, with a measurements CSV and a thumbnail. Open it with File › Open…: FluoroView finds the image at its last known path or next to the .fv, or asks you for it, and checks it against a content fingerprint. If the image already has work, you choose to Replace it (your current work is backed up first) or Merge.

Figures

⇧⌘E exports the selected region's box or the current view as the composite plus one labelled panel per visible channel, side by side, with one scale bar. Scale bar, channel names, region outlines and note pins can each be burned in. Formats are PNG, 8-bit RGB TIFF at the DPI you choose, and the raw 16-bit area as OME-TIFF. Figures are full resolution up to 8,000 px on the long side; larger areas are reduced by exact 2×2 averaging.
